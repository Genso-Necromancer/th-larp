These are the different terrain planned for the game, and what category they fall in. A sprite sheet would have all or most of these, and you can make different sprite sheets for each biome. As long as they're placed in the same locations on the sheet every time(even if something is intentionally left out, reserve it's spot with empty space) and we can easily swap the sprite sheet with minimal effort.

# Base Terrain.
These are on the base layer for maps, and can have mods placed upon it. Final values and effects are the combination for Base + Mod's values. Unless an exception is stated. These are all on their own sprite sheet.
- `Flat`: provides nothing. this is dirt, the flat land, the basic indoor tiles, almost featureless save for color and patterns
- `Ground`: small Graze bonus. This is your grasslands, natural tiles, hardly a step up from Flat
- `River`: Good Graze bonus as niche for swimmers. Narrow water, cuts through terrain, can be passed through with enough natural movement, outdoor varients are rivers and indoor varients are channels(work in progress name?) Not a mod tile, but needs to interact with the edges of other terrain. Can't be a mod tile, because bridges are mod tiles and need to go over top them.
- `Water`: Great Graze bonus. This is open water. your seas, your lakes, maybe the depper center flanked by river tiles to create "big rivers". Also not a mod tile, but also needs it's own edge interaction.
- `Sanzu`: Right now it's the same as water mechanically, but it's not meant to be. It's dangerous water, not because it's rough waters. It's Asia's River Styx. Lost souls exist in the Sanzu and it's crossed to reach your judgement in the after life. However great your sins determines how long it takes to fairy across it. In use, it'd be more like a river tile with special properties and needs to stand out and reflect that it's the Sanzu.
- `HotSpring`: Small single water pools. It could technically work as a mod tile if need be? didn't cross my mind until now. It's a special tile that gets place on it's own rather than worrying about connecting to others. It's purpose is working as a slow, passive, composure restoring effect. Like standing on Forts in Fire Emblem, and will cost money like in Sigurd's game.
- `Rough`: Minor Graze and Def bonus. Also known as "Brush". Costs extra movement to get through. It's the next tier up terrain defense wise. It's primarily intended as the underbrush to outdoor enviornments. Able to be placed on it's own, or as the base tile under trees for deeper forests and compounding bonuses and movement penalties a like.
- `OpenRough`: Small Penalty to Graze. This is difficult terrain that works against you. Snow, sand, beaches, any uneven ground that slows your movement, leaves you open and difficult to fight on.
- `HellSand`: Currently has bonuses same as Rough. This is place holder. it should be penalizing. The underworld "upgrade" to `OpenRough`. It's like my old terrain gimmick in D&D where the sand had the vague faces of the damned faintly in it and it unnaturally pulled on anyone walking through it. Alot like the Sanzu in a way, where it's taken a natural terrain that's been established and making in far more punishing and unnatural looking.

# Mod Terrain
Layered atop ground, and best practice would be to design them like .png photoshop templates. Where their "blending" with the ground is such that you can swap the under tile and it still looks right. Like base terrain, these have a seperate sprite sheet.
- `Fort`: Big Def and Graze. Some movement penalty. Just like forts in FE. My shitty placeholder fort asset shows a pretty good example of how I imagine them designed. Contained to single tiles, even if you have multiple next to one another.
- `Bridge`: Leaves you open, penalizes Graze. Also makes camping Bridges more difficult. Unlike other Mod tiles, Bridges negate the values of whatever tile is beneath them, using only it's values. It's a bridge.
- `Hill`: Small Graze bonus and some Hit, move penalties. High ground basically. These are elevated tiles, not quite "mountains" which I suppose I should have added a mountain tile. You've seen them in Fire emblem. It'd be difficult to make these universal to base tiles if you try to match a base tiles color. You could do what FE has done in the past and make them their own color and then do the usual blending trick at it's edges.
- `Woodland`: Good Graze Bonus, small def bonus, move penalties. it's trees, which even those can have varients. You have outdoors that look like woods, snow trees in any biome with snow, more organized trees in town maps and dead trees for hell/underworld biomes. Doubtful of it's use for indoor maps. 
- `House`: Just like FE, these function nearly the same. Possibly even a visit mechanic. Currently there's no "towns" like in FE where brigands attack or you can visit to close their gates. These would function in that role as well, and need a destroyed varient and a "visited" varient. As well as the classic roof color difference for "has item" and "doesn't have item."
- `Shrine`: This is like churches in Sigurd's game. Spend money perturn passively and get Life back slowly. Similar to hotspring in that rigard. This is Touhou, so torri gates, Shintoism, they should be themed that way. Outdoor versions should look like the whole shrine set up, outdoor maps have always been more "zoomed out". Though indoor, or town biomes could still utilize this tile where instead they're more like small shrines than buildings. I'd highly recommend looking up reference images of Japanese Shinto shrines and the sort of shrines they have within cities.
- `Shop`: Item and Weapon varients need to exist. In my test assets I just reused the house asset, but put a blue pot and a sign outfront. You could reuse the house tile, add one or two "shop" details like that pot, give them a different color roof and I kinda like the idea of the sign. Make it a little bigger than would be "realistic" and put a single symbol on it. Item Bag or a Sword to indicate which it is.

# Wall Terrain
Basically Mod tiles, but have their own sprite sheet. They are intensive enough that it's best to keep them to their own sheet. Walls need to have different angles and directions like building blocks for creating different wall lay outs. 
Godot has a feature where I can connect them internally in a way so I can brush the walls and they will automatically swap to the correct tiles based on what edges touch. Infact I can do that for *all* of these and that could help immensely for making things fit nicely.
Each tile can have a varient placed beside it and internally function as the original with little differences. Walls currently make use of that feature for broken and breakable walls to swap between. Same tech is used on the next group for doors/chests.
If you run walls through the middle of terrain, it'd be easier to get all the different directions. For outdoor varients, if you put "towers" on the ends of said walls they can then overlap and mask eachother to create the illusion of being continuous. This is just what I learned from my tests sprites. Indoor varients can't use that trick obviously, but you could figure something out.
Side note: I considered walls acting as rims to hexes, but haven't done visual tests of this to see if that'd be easier or harder and if it'd look terrible or not.

- `Wall`: Practically impassable. No way to shoot over, through or fly over it. Outdoor varients might have roofs over layed like in FE when the inside is hidden?(not implemented) Indoor varients are way easier to convey as black space can help implying the existence of a roof they're leading up to.
- `WallShoot`: These are walls that can only be shot through. Either murder holes, windows, or some other reason that ranged attacks can get through, but no way to fly over them. You could change up what makes ranged attacks possible with different biome varients if you want.
- `WallFly`: These are your most basic of walls. Can shoot over them, fly over them, typically intended for Outdoor and Town style biomes. Doubt it would be crucial for indoor maps.
- Breakable varients: Your standard breakable wall asset. Right now it's a wall tile on the same sprite sheet. It uses a full wall asset but changed to look cracked or damaged. With the varient tile set to be a broken wall that's collapsed in the middle. Right now I'm getting an idea as I type that might cut down on your workload. For "breakable" I could easily make a third layer and you'd just need a sprite sheet that lines up with existing walls that is basically a "breakable effect" sticker to slap ontop of them. What's uncertain is the "broken" varient. If you were to make a "broken" sticker like the breakable, the wall would be seen as still standing under the decal of being "broken". This, of course, *wouldn't* happen if it's possible for me to somehow layer mask the parts you want transparent? hell, if I unlocked that tech it'd open *alot* of doors for you when making these and cut down on some workload. I'll get back to you about this.

# Interactive Tiles
Like Walls, it's essentially another sort of Mod tile. Not the same layer, but not intended to be used with a mod tile. Only a base. These all have a closed and open varient.
- `Door`: Currently uses an entire wall asset, but with a door. The test asset actually uses black pixels inside rather than showing through. Which means it could work as a decal with different angles and slapped onto any wall. Given they're already a different layer, it's entirely possible. Though if I discover how Godot layer masking works, and it works how we hope, you could have the other side seen through at straight-on angles.
- `Chest`: It's a chest.
